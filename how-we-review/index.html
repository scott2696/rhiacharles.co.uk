The five weights

My scoring weights, applied identically to every operator on this site
CriterionWeight What it measures
Payout speed and reliability30%Timed from the moment I press withdraw to the moment cleared funds show in my account or wallet. Every request goes in the ledger, including the slow ones.
Bonus honesty25%What the welcome offer actually costs to clear, modelled from the operator's own wagering terms — not the size of the number in the advert.
Cashier and GBP banking20%Whether pounds go in and out without a currency conversion, how many UK methods work, minimum and maximum withdrawal limits, and any operator fee.
Game range and providers15%Studio count, whether the big providers are the real feeds, live dealer depth, and how the library behaves on a phone.
Support and account handling10%Live chat response times measured at three times of day, KYC turnaround, and how the account behaves when something goes wrong.

Why these weights and not others

Payout speed carries 30% because it is the only criterion that becomes the whole experience when it goes wrong. A casino with a beautiful lobby and a broken cashier is not a casino; it is a deposit box. It is also the criterion competitors are least willing to measure, because measuring it requires actually depositing money.

Bonus honesty carries 25% because that is where an uninformed player loses the most money fastest, and because it is genuinely measurable rather than a matter of taste. The score comes from the clearance model — turnover required, multiplied by the house edge, against the bonus value — not from how large the advertised percentage is. An operator advertising 1000% at 10x scores better than one advertising 600% at 35x, which is the opposite of how most rankings behave.

Cashier and GBP banking carries 20% because a forced currency conversion costs about 3% in each direction, which is worse than almost any fee, and because a UK player being unable to deposit at all is a complete failure regardless of everything else.

Game range carries 15% — deliberately low. Every site in this market has thousands of games and the marginal value of the four thousandth slot is nil. What this criterion actually captures is whether the big studios' real feeds are present rather than clones, whether the live floor is genuinely staffed in UK evening hours, and whether the lobby is usable on a phone.

Support carries 10%, the smallest weight, because good support is a nice-to-have when everything works and no substitute for a functioning cashier when it does not. It is scored on measured live-chat response times at three times of day and on KYC turnaround from the ledger.

The test protocol, step by step

  1. Register from a UK connection with real details. No VPN, real name, real Bristol address, real date of birth. A site that refuses a UK address comes off the list at this step and goes on the rejected pile.
  2. Read the entire terms document before depositing. Not the summary box. I record the maximum bet while wagering, the maximum cashout, the game weighting table, the bonus expiry, whether the bonus is sticky, the withdrawal minimums and maximums, and any fee.
  3. Verify identity immediately, from the account settings, before depositing. This tests whether verification works at all and records exactly what is asked for — the data behind the KYC diary in the ledger.
  4. Deposit £100 by debit card and £100 by a second method — open banking, an e-wallet or crypto — so the cashier can be compared across routes.
  5. Take the welcome bonus on one deposit and decline it on the other. This is the only way to see how the site behaves both with and without a wagering condition attached.
  6. Play. Slots and live tables, on desktop and on a phone, at varying stakes. Check the RTP shown in the game info panel against the studio's published figure for at least fifteen popular titles.
  7. Request at least five withdrawals, at different amounts between £40 and £2,100 and at different times of day and week, timing each one from the confirm button to cleared funds.
  8. Message live chat three times — once around 10am, once around 8pm, once after midnight — with a specific question that has a right answer, such as the maximum bet permitted while a bonus is active. Time the reply and judge whether it is an answer or a link.
  9. Hold the account for at least eight weeks to see what the site does after the welcome offer is spent: whether promotions recur, whether terms change, whether the account is restricted.
  10. Score, publish, and log the raw data in the ledger.

The whole cycle takes about two months per operator, which is why ten sites are ranked rather than fifty.

What disqualifies a site outright

These are not scored down. They mean no score at all, and the site goes on the rejected list on my main ranking page with its reason.

  • No verifiable licence. The footer badge must lead to a live entry on the issuing regulator's own register, naming the operating company. Five of the 42 sites I opened this year failed this.
  • A withdrawal delayed beyond seven days without explanation. Six sites. Four of them paid eventually; it should not take a week and a chase.
  • Bonus terms materially different from the advertised summary. Seven sites, usually a maximum cashout buried in the full document that capped a headline offer at a couple of hundred pounds.
  • Forced currency conversion on GBP deposits. Three sites. Deposit in pounds, balance in euros, roughly 3% skimmed each way.
  • Support unreachable or scripted to uselessness across three attempts at three times of day. Three sites.
  • Predatory reverse-withdrawal design — a long pending window with a prominent one-tap button that returns your own withdrawal to your playable balance. Two sites.
  • Marketing aimed explicitly at self-excluded players. One site. Not on here at any commission rate.

I do not publish the names of rejected operators. A one-person publisher naming operators invites correspondence I have neither the budget nor the appetite for, and the categories are the useful part anyway — they are the failure modes, and you can apply them yourself to any site you are considering.

The limits of this method

An honest method includes its own weaknesses:

  • The samples are small. Five to eleven withdrawals per operator is enough to see a pattern and not enough to be statistically confident. Where a sample is at the low end, the ledger says so.
  • I am one person in one place. Everything is tested from a Bristol home connection with one UK bank and one set of documents. A different bank, a different card issuer or a different network could behave differently.
  • I am a disciplined customer. I read every term before claiming, never breach a maximum bet and never hold duplicate accounts. That is exactly the profile least likely to have a withdrawal refused, so “zero refusals across 74 requests” says less about the operators than it looks.
  • Past behaviour does not bind future behaviour. An operator that paid me eleven times is under no obligation to pay you. Ownership changes, licences lapse, and offshore brands disappear.
  • Prices and terms move constantly. Every figure on this site was accurate on 4 September 2026 and some of it will be wrong by the time you read it. Always check current terms at the cashier.

How each timing was taken

Consistency matters more than precision here, so the protocol is fixed:

  • Start: the moment I press the confirm button on the withdrawal request, recorded to the minute from the device clock.
  • Stop: the moment cleared, spendable funds appear — a confirmed transaction in the receiving wallet, a credited e-wallet balance, or an available balance in my bank account. Not the moment the casino marks the request “approved”, which is usually much earlier and is the figure operators quote.
  • Conditions: home broadband in Bristol, no VPN, real name, real address, real UK bank account.
  • Amounts: between £40 and £2,100, deliberately varied, because a casino that pays £60 quickly and stalls £900 is a slow casino.
  • Times of day: spread across weekday mornings, weekday evenings and weekends, because approval queues are staffed in office hours somewhere and the difference is large.
  • Everything is logged, including the requests that went badly. Nothing is excluded as an outlier.

The tip that saves you a day

Every operator here triggered verification at first withdrawal rather than at registration, which puts the delay at exactly the moment you least want it. All eight accept documents from the account settings menu at any time. Upload photo ID, a selfie and a proof of address dated within three months on the day you open the account and your first payout runs at the speed of the payment rail rather than the speed of a compliance queue. Two practical notes from the rejections above: photograph documents flat with all four corners visible, and use a council tax or utility bill rather than a mobile phone bill, which is refused surprisingly often.

What 74 payouts have taught me

  1. The method matters more than the casino. Median crypto payout was 2 hours 55 minutes; median debit card payout was two working days. That is a sixteen-fold difference, and it dwarfs the difference between the fastest and slowest operators.
  2. The first withdrawal is always the slow one. Across all eleven operators, the first payout took roughly four times the median of every subsequent payout at the same site, because verification lands on it.
  3. Time of day matters more than it should. Weekday morning requests cleared roughly twice as fast as Friday evening requests of the same size at the same casino. Approval queues are staffed by people, in an office, somewhere.
  4. Advertised ranges are aspirational. Six of the eleven operators were slower than the bottom of their own advertised range at least once. None was catastrophically slower.
  5. Nothing was refused. Seventy-four requests, zero refusals, no forfeited balances and no disputes. That is genuinely reassuring, and it is also a modest sample from a disciplined player who read every set of terms before claiming anything. It is not a promise about your account.
  6. Third parties are the hidden delay. An e-wallet is a second compliance gate. A card scheme is a settlement cycle nobody can compress. Where I could remove a middleman, the payout got faster.

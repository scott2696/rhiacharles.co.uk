What each welcome bonus actually costs to clear

This is the section every other “best online casinos” page leaves out, and it is the one that will save you the most money.

A casino bonus is not a gift. It is a loan of chips with a turnover condition attached, and the condition has a price. If a bonus carries 35x wagering, you have to stake the bonus thirty-five times over before the balance becomes withdrawable cash — and every one of those stakes is exposed to the house edge. On a slot running at 96% return to player, the house keeps 4p of every pound you turn over. Multiply that by the turnover the terms demand and you get the real cost of the offer.

How to read a wagering requirement in five seconds

Multiply the wagering number by the bonus. That is your turnover. Take 4% of it. That is roughly what the bonus costs you on slots. If the answer is bigger than the bonus, the offer is a marketing number, not money. A 35x bonus is break-even at about 25x, so anything above 25x is, on average, working against you — which is precisely why almost every offer you see advertised sits at 35x or 40x.

Two caveats I want to be straight about. First, this model assumes 96% RTP, and plenty of slots run lower — sometimes the same slot runs lower, because many studios ship several RTP builds of one game and the operator chooses which to license. Second, expectation is not a promise: variance means individual outcomes scatter widely either side of these figures, and the reason casinos are profitable is that the scatter resolves in their favour over enough hands. What the table gives you is the direction of travel, applied consistently, which is more than an advertised percentage gives you.

Is it legal to play at these casinos in the UK?

Yes, for you. Not necessarily for them. That distinction is the whole of the answer and almost nobody states it plainly, so here it is.

Under the Gambling Act 2005, the offence of providing facilities for gambling to consumers in Great Britain without a licence sits with the operator, not the player. There is no provision anywhere in the Act, and no UK Gambling Commission licence condition, that makes it an offence for an adult in Britain to open an account with an offshore casino, deposit money or withdraw winnings. You are not breaking the law by playing at any site on this page. Nobody has ever been prosecuted for it, because there is no offence to prosecute.

What is true is that a casino accepting British customers without a UKGC licence is operating unlawfully as far as the Commission is concerned, and the Commission's response is to disrupt it — asking payment providers to decline transactions, asking search engines and advertisers to delist it, and issuing cease-and-desist notices. That disruption is aimed at the operator, but you feel the effects: a card deposit that fails for no stated reason, an account that suddenly stops accepting UK sign-ups, or a site that closes to British players at a week's notice with your balance in it.

The tax position is simpler and more cheerful: gambling winnings are not taxable income in the United Kingdom, whether you win them at a UKGC-licensed site or an offshore one. There is no tax to declare and no threshold above which that changes. The full detail, including the rare cases where HMRC takes a different view, is on my tax on gambling winnings page, and the regulatory picture is set out in full on UK gambling laws.

Are online casinos legal in the UK?

UKGC-licensed online casinos are fully legal. Offshore casinos that accept British customers without a UK Gambling Commission licence are operating unlawfully from the Commission's point of view, but the offence under the Gambling Act 2005 sits with the operator, not with you. There is no law making it an offence for an adult in Britain to open an account, deposit or withdraw at an offshore casino, and nobody has been prosecuted for doing so. What you lose is the protection: no GamStop, no IBAS complaints route, no funds segregation requirement and no UKGC stake or speed limits.

Do I pay tax on casino winnings in the UK?

No. Gambling winnings are not treated as taxable income in the United Kingdom for players, and that applies equally to UKGC-licensed and offshore sites. There is no threshold, nothing to declare on a self-assessment return, and no difference between a £50 win and a £50,000 one. Two things to be aware of: interest earned on winnings sitting in a savings account is taxable in the normal way, and if you buy cryptocurrency to fund an account, disposing of that crypto can create a capital gains event independent of the gambling itself.

What does a 35x wagering requirement actually mean?

It means you must stake the bonus thirty-five times over before the balance becomes withdrawable. A £100 bonus at 35x requires £3,500 of turnover. On a slot returning 96%, that turnover costs you about £140 in expected losses to unlock a £100 bonus, so the offer is worth roughly minus £40 to you on average. Break-even sits at about 25x. That is why I model the cost of every welcome offer on this site rather than quoting the headline percentage: a 600% bonus at 35x is worth less than a 100% bonus at 10x.

What is the difference between a casino's RTP and its house edge?

They are the same number from opposite ends. Return to player is the share of everything staked that a game pays back over millions of spins; the house edge is what remains. A slot at 96% RTP has a 4% house edge, meaning it keeps 4p of every pound staked on average. Two things people miss: the figure is a long-run average and tells you nothing about one session, and the same slot title is often shipped in several RTP builds — 96.5%, 94% and 92% versions of one game — with the operator choosing which to run. Reputable sites publish the build they use in the game info panel. Check it.

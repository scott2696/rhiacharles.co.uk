The legal position

Written by Olivia Williams. General information, not legal advice.

Section 33 of the Gambling Act 2005 places the licensing offence on the operator providing facilities for gambling in Great Britain, not on the customer. Betting with an offshore bookmaker is not an offence for a UK resident, and there is no provision under which a punter could be prosecuted for it.

Betting duty was moved from punters to operators in 2001, so winnings are not taxable. That holds regardless of licensing jurisdiction, and it holds even for people who bet full time, because established case law does not treat gambling as a trade. See tax on gambling winnings.

What differs is the enforcement environment. The Commission disrupts unlicensed operators through payment providers and advertising networks, which shows up as declined card deposits and occasional abrupt withdrawal from the UK market. And your contract is governed by the operator's terms under a foreign law, with a foreign regulator as the only escalation. For a disputed £300 settlement, that is in practice no remedy at all.

Six rules if you use one anyway

  1. Set a deposit limit in the account settings on day one.
  2. Verify your identity immediately, so a winning bet does not sit behind a compliance queue.
  3. Compare the overround on the market you are about to bet, not the welcome offer. One is worth money every week; the other happens once.

Questions

What are betting sites not on GamStop?

Bookmakers that hold no UK Gambling Commission licence, usually licensed in Curaçao or Anjouan instead. GamStop participation is a UKGC licence condition, so these books are not connected to it and cannot see a UK self-exclusion. The same absence removes best odds guaranteed, enhanced each-way terms, IBAS dispute resolution, affordability checks and any requirement to segregate customer funds.

Is it legal to bet with a site not on GamStop?

Yes, for you. Section 33 of the Gambling Act 2005 makes unlicensed provision of gambling facilities an offence committed by the operator, not by the customer, and no player has ever been prosecuted for using an offshore bookmaker. Winnings remain free of tax. What changes is protection: no GamStop, no IBAS, and a disputed settlement that has to be escalated to a regulator overseas.

Do non-GamStop bookmakers offer best odds guaranteed?

Not reliably — none of the seven books I tested offered it consistently. Best odds guaranteed pays you at the starting price if the horse you backed at an early price drifts, and it is standard at UK Gambling Commission licensed bookmakers because UK racing punters expect it. Its absence is the single biggest practical loss for a regular racing bettor and it will usually cost more over a season than any welcome offer returns.

Bank gambling blocks — the best free control there is

If you take one thing from this page, take this. A bank gambling block stops transactions coded as gambling at the payment level, which means it works regardless of whether the operator is licensed in Britain or in the Indian Ocean. It does not depend on a database, a list of domains, or your willpower at 11pm.

The cooling-off period is the whole point. A block you can switch off in the moment you want to switch it off is not a block; a block with 48 hours of friction between the impulse and the deposit almost always outlasts the impulse.

One gap worth knowing: a bank block stops card and direct payments coded as gambling. It does not stop a transfer to a cryptocurrency exchange, because that transaction is not coded as gambling. If crypto is a route you have used, pair the bank block with BetBlocker or Gamban, which block the casino sites themselves.

GamStop, and what it does not cover

GamStop is free, run by a not-for-profit, and takes a few minutes. You register once, choose six months, one year or five years, and every operator holding a UK Gambling Commission remote licence must check the database and refuse you an account.

What it does well: it is comprehensive across the licensed British market, it cannot be cancelled early, and after the term expires exclusion continues until you actively ask for it to be lifted, with a cooling-off period after that request.

What it does not cover: any operator without a UKGC licence. That is not a loophole, it is the limit of the Commission's jurisdiction — a regulator cannot compel a company in Curaçao to query a British database. Every operator featured elsewhere on this website is in that category. GamStop will not stop you reaching them, and this is exactly why BetBlocker or Gamban belongs alongside it rather than instead of it.

BetBlocker and Gamban

BetBlocker is free, run by a registered charity, and blocks tens of thousands of gambling domains including offshore operators. It installs on phones, tablets and computers, works across Windows, macOS, Android and iOS, and once you set a restriction period it cannot be removed until that period ends — not even by you, which is the feature. You can also set daily time windows if a complete block is more than you want. There is no registration and no personal data required.

Gamban is a paid product, though it is frequently provided free through the National Gambling Treatment Service and by some operators. It blocks gambling sites and apps across devices and is deliberately difficult to remove.

Either is effective. If cost is a factor at all, use BetBlocker — it is free, it is genuinely good, and the fact that it is free means there is no reason to delay installing it while you decide.

Deposit limits and account tools

Every operator featured on this site offers these, and almost nobody uses them:

  • Deposit limits — daily, weekly or monthly. Set one the day you open an account, before you have any reason to want it higher. Reductions take effect immediately; increases should be delayed, and at a well-run operator they are.
  • Loss limits — a cap on net losses over a period. Better than a deposit limit, because it accounts for money you win and then restake.
  • Session time limits and reality checks — a pop-up every 30 or 60 minutes telling you how long you have played. Modest, and better than nothing.
  • Time-outs — a cooling-off period of 24 hours to six weeks. Useful for interrupting a run rather than stopping altogether.
  • Self-exclusion at that operator — six months upwards, irreversible during the term, and usually applied across the operator's sister brands too.
  • Withdrawal locking, where offered — prevents a pending withdrawal being cancelled and returned to your balance. Switch it on. The absence of this feature, and its aggressive opposite, was enough to disqualify two of the 42 sites tested this year.

Signs worth taking seriously

Not a diagnosis, and not a checklist to score yourself against. But if several of these are familiar, the helpline number is free:

  • Gambling with money set aside for something else — rent, bills, food.
  • Chasing losses: betting more to recover what you have lost.
  • Gambling with borrowed money, or on a credit card.
  • Hiding it — deleting apps before someone sees, lying about amounts, using a separate account.
  • Thinking about it when you are not doing it, or planning the next session during the current one.
  • Needing larger stakes to feel the same interest.
  • Restlessness or irritability when you cannot play.
  • Relief rather than enjoyment when you win.
  • Missing work, sleep or people because of it.
  • Trying to stop, and finding you cannot.

The most reliable single indicator is simpler than any of those: if you are keeping the amount secret from someone whose opinion you care about, that is information.

The arithmetic nobody puts in an advert

Since this is a site about gambling, here is the arithmetic that governs all of it, stated plainly.

Every casino game has a permanent mathematical margin in the operator's favour, between about 0.5% on blackjack played to basic strategy and 10% or more on some slots. That margin applies to everything you stake, not everything you deposit — and because winnings get restaked, turnover is usually many times deposits.

The practical formula: expected hourly cost = stake per spin × spins per hour × house edge.

And the game you choose changes the cost by a factor of eighty at the same stake — which is why the UK Gambling Commission caps slot stakes at £5, requires a 2.5-second minimum spin and bans autoplay at licensed sites. If it is somebody else

Living with someone else's gambling is its own kind of difficult, and there is specific support for it.

  • The National Gambling Helpline supports affected others as well as gamblers: 0808 8020 133.
  • Gam-Anon runs peer support groups for family and friends.
  • GamFam provides resources specifically for families.
  • StepChange gives free debt advice where gambling has caused debt.

Two practical things that help. Protect the household finances first — separate accounts, gambling blocks on shared cards, and no lending to cover losses, which sustains the pattern rather than ending it. And understand that you cannot install a block on somebody else's behalf and expect it to hold; the tools work when the person chooses them, which is why getting them to make one phone call is worth more than any amount of monitoring.

Questions

What is the most effective way to stop gambling?

Combining three free tools rather than relying on one: a bank gambling block, which stops the payment regardless of where the operator is licensed; BetBlocker or Gamban, which block the sites themselves including offshore ones; and GamStop, which stops you opening accounts at UK-licensed operators. Together they close almost every casual route. Add a call to the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133, which is free and confidential, because tools work better alongside support than instead of it.

Does GamStop block casinos not on GamStop?

No, and it cannot. GamStop is a condition of a UK Gambling Commission licence, so it binds only operators that hold one. A casino licensed in Curaçao or Anjouan is outside the Commission's jurisdiction and cannot be compelled to check a British database. To block offshore sites you need BetBlocker, which is free and charity-run, or Gamban, which is often free through the NHS treatment service. Both work where GamStop cannot.

Can I cancel a GamStop self-exclusion early?

No. Once a GamStop registration is active it runs for the full term you chose — six months, one year or five years — and cannot be shortened. After the term expires, the exclusion continues until you actively contact GamStop to reinstate your accounts, and there is a further cooling-off period after that request. That irreversibility is the feature, not a fault.

How do I set a gambling block with my bank?

It is in your banking app, usually under card settings or spending controls. Monzo, Starling, Lloyds, Halifax, NatWest, RBS, Barclays, HSBC, First Direct, Santander, Nationwide, TSB and Revolut all offer one, and it is free. Switching it on is immediate; switching it off carries a cooling-off period of 24 to 72 hours, which is deliberate. It blocks transactions coded as gambling at any merchant, so unlike GamStop it works at offshore operators too.

Is BetBlocker really free?

Yes. BetBlocker is provided free by a registered charity, requires no registration and collects no personal data. It blocks tens of thousands of gambling domains, including offshore operators, across Windows, macOS, Android and iOS, and once you set a restriction period it cannot be removed until that period ends. If cost has been a reason to put off installing blocking software, it is not a reason here.

How much gambling is too much?

There is no universal figure, because the amount matters less than the relationship. The practical test is not how much you spend but whether you are still in control of it: are you gambling with money meant for something else, chasing losses, borrowing, hiding it, or trying to stop and finding you cannot? A useful marker is secrecy — if you are keeping the amount from someone whose opinion you care about, that is worth acting on regardless of the number.

Where can I get free treatment for gambling harm in the UK?

The NHS National Gambling Treatment Service runs free specialist clinics across England, including the National Problem Gambling Clinic, and you can self-refer through nhs.uk without going through a GP. GamCare provides free counselling nationally via the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133. Gordon Moody offers free residential treatment for severe harm, and Gamblers Anonymous runs peer support meetings across the UK, in person and online.

Can I get my money back from a gambling site?

Generally no. Losses from gambling you chose to do are not recoverable, and a chargeback claim over losing money has no basis because a gambling loss is not a faulty product. The exception is where an operator broke its own obligations — allowing a self-excluded customer to gamble at a UKGC-licensed site, for example — which can be taken to the operator and then to its ADR provider. At an offshore operator there is no comparable route, which is one of the real costs of playing outside the UK system.

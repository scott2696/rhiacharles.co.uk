What RTP actually is, and what it is not

Return to player is the proportion of total stakes a game returns to players across an enormous number of plays — typically the studio simulates billions of spins to certify it. A 96% slot returns 96p per pound staked over that horizon. The remaining 4p is the house edge.

Three things RTP is not, and all three trip people up:

  • It is not a session prediction. Over a hundred spins, a 96% slot can return 300% or 0%. RTP converges only over hundreds of thousands of spins, far more than any individual will ever play on one title. Do not treat it as a forecast.
  • It is not a promise the game is “due”. Each spin is independent. A slot that has paid nothing for two hours is not owed anything, and one that has just paid a large win is not going cold. The gambler's fallacy is the single most expensive belief in this industry.
  • It is not the same as your realised return. Your realised return depends on which builds you played, how the bonus terms weighted your games, and pure chance. RTP is the price tag, not the receipt.

What RTP is good for is comparison. Between two otherwise identical evenings, the one played at 96.5% costs you a third less than the one played at 94.5%. Over a year of regular play, that is a real amount of money, and it is entirely within your control.

Why high RTP and high volatility are different things

These get confused constantly and they measure completely different properties.

RTP is how much comes back. Volatility is how lumpily it comes back. A low-volatility slot at 96% pays small amounts often; a high-volatility slot at the same 96% pays almost nothing for long stretches and then very occasionally pays a great deal. Over infinite spins they return the same. Over your actual bankroll they behave nothing alike.

Which to choose depends on what you are doing:

  • Clearing a wagering requirement? Low volatility, high RTP. You need to survive the turnover, not win big during it. A high-volatility slot will frequently take your whole balance before the requirement is met.
  • Playing for entertainment on a small budget? Low to medium volatility. Your session lasts longer for the same money.
  • Chasing a single large win and content to lose the stake? High volatility. Just be honest with yourself that the median outcome is losing everything you put in.

Why site-wide payout percentages are meaningless

You will see pages ranking casinos by a “97.4% payout rate”. Three reasons to ignore that number entirely:

  1. It is a weighted average of what players happened to play. A month when players favoured blackjack produces a much higher figure than a month when they favoured high-volatility slots, with no change whatever to the games on offer.
  2. It is almost never independently audited at offshore sites. Where a UKGC or MGA operator might publish an eCOGRA-verified figure, an offshore operator generally publishes a number it produced itself.
  3. It tells you nothing actionable. You cannot play the site average. You play individual games, at individual builds, and those are the numbers that determine what your evening costs.

That is why the ranking at the top of this page is built on RTP transparency — does the operator show you the figure for the game you are about to play — rather than on a self-reported average.

What is a good RTP for an online slot?

96% or higher is the sensible threshold for a mainstream video slot. Between 94% and 96% is common and playable but noticeably more expensive. Below 94% you are paying a premium of at least £20 per £1,000 staked over a standard game for no additional benefit. A handful of slots run above 97% — Blood Suckers at 98% is the best-known — and table games run higher still, with blackjack played to basic strategy at about 99.5%.

Can casinos change the RTP of a slot?

An operator cannot alter a certified game's maths on the fly, but it can choose which certified build to license, and many studios ship the same title at several RTPs. Starburst exists at 96.09% and 90.05%, Book of Dead at 96.21% and 94.25%. Switching between builds is a commercial configuration decision, not a mid-session manipulation, and reputable operators disclose the figure in the game info panel. Sites that do not disclose it are the reason this page exists.

Does a higher RTP mean I will win more often?

It means you will lose less over a long period, which is not the same thing. Frequency of wins is governed by volatility, not RTP. A high-volatility slot at 97% will pay you far less often than a low-volatility slot at 95%, while returning more in total across hundreds of thousands of spins. If you want longer sessions from the same money, choose high RTP and low volatility together.

Is RTP the same as the house edge?

They are the same number viewed from either end. House edge = 100% minus RTP. A 96% slot has a 4% house edge, meaning it keeps 4p of every pound staked on average. The practical difference is which one makes the cost obvious: 96% sounds generous, while “this game charges 4% of everything you stake, repeatedly” describes the same fact more usefully.

How much does RTP matter over one evening?

Less than people think in the short run, and much more than they think over a year. Across a single hundred-spin session, chance dominates completely and a 94% slot may well outperform a 97% one. Across a year of regular play, the gap is close to arithmetic: a £1-a-spin player turning over £20,000 in a year pays £800 at 96% and £1,200 at 94%. The £400 difference is not luck, it is the price of not checking the info panel.

Do live dealer games have RTP?

Yes, and they are usually far better than slots. Live blackjack played with basic strategy runs at about 99.5%, baccarat's banker bet at 98.94% and European roulette at 97.30%. The catch is that live games are typically weighted at 10% or 0% towards bonus wagering requirements, so they cannot be used to clear a welcome offer efficiently. Play them with an unbonused balance and they are the cheapest entertainment in the building.

What GamStop actually is

GamStop is a free national self-exclusion scheme run by a not-for-profit company. You register once, choose a period of six months, one year or five years, and every operator holding a UK Gambling Commission remote licence is required by its licence conditions to check the database and refuse you an account.

Some facts that get muddled:

  • It covers UKGC-licensed operators only. That is not a loophole; it is the limit of the Commission's jurisdiction. An offshore operator is not bound by UK licence conditions and cannot be compelled to query a UK database.
  • It cannot be cancelled early. Once the minimum period is running, it runs. After it expires, exclusion continues until you actively contact GamStop to reactivate your accounts, and there is a cooling-off period after that.
  • It works on registered details. Name, date of birth, address, email, mobile, postcode. Registering with different details at a UKGC site is a breach of that operator's terms.
  • It does not block gambling advertising, websites, or apps. It refuses accounts. That is a meaningful difference, and it is why blocking software is a useful companion to it rather than a substitute.

The legal position, precisely

This section was written by Olivia Williams, who practised as a regulatory solicitor. It is general information, not legal advice.

Under section 33 of the Gambling Act 2005, it is an offence to provide facilities for gambling to consumers in Great Britain without an operating licence. The offence is committed by the operator. There is no corresponding offence of using an unlicensed operator, and none has ever been created. A player in Britain who opens an account with an offshore casino, deposits, plays and withdraws commits no offence, and no prosecution of a player has ever occurred because there is nothing to prosecute.

Two consequences follow that people often miss.

First, the Commission's enforcement is aimed at the operator but lands on you. The Commission works with payment providers, search engines, advertising networks and hosting companies to disrupt unlicensed operators serving Britain. In practice that means card deposits that fail without explanation, sites that stop accepting UK registrations at short notice, and occasionally an operator that withdraws from the British market while customers still hold balances. That last one is a real risk, not a theoretical one.

Second, your contractual position is weak. Your relationship with the casino is a contract governed by its terms, usually under the law of its licensing jurisdiction, with disputes going first to the operator and then to a regulator thousands of miles away. There is no UK small claims route that is practical for a £500 disputed withdrawal against a Curaçao company. That is why the evidence on this website is payout data rather than reassurance.

On tax: gambling winnings are not taxable income for players in the UK, and that is unaffected by where the operator is licensed. See tax on gambling winnings. The broader framework is on UK gambling laws.

Who these sites are genuinely for

Being straightforward about it, there are three groups.

People who never self-excluded and have left the UK market for other reasons. The commonest is affordability checks: a UKGC operator asking for payslips and bank statements before allowing a £300 deposit is a genuine irritation for somebody who can comfortably afford it, and some players leave over it. Others want stakes above £5 a spin, or bonus sizes UK operators cannot offer. These are adults making an informed trade and this website is written for them.

People who self-excluded and now want back in. That is the group the section at the top of this page is for. Please read it.

People who did not realise there was a difference. If you arrived here from a search for “best online casinos” without knowing that these sites sit outside the UK system, then the honest recommendation is to consider a UKGC-licensed operator first. You will get smaller bonuses, lower stake limits and more intrusive checks — and GamStop, IBAS, segregated funds and a regulator in Birmingham that answers the phone. For a lot of people that is the better product, and no commission is worth burying it.

If you are going to use one anyway, do these six things

  1. Set a deposit limit on day one, in the account settings, before you have any reason to want it higher. Every site I rank offers it.
  2. Verify your identity immediately — photo ID, selfie, proof of address dated within three months. It removes the delay at withdrawal and it confirms the operator's checks work at all.
  3. Check the weekly withdrawal cap before you play. A £20,000 win at a £6,000-a-week site takes a month to pay out, and during that month the money is sitting inside a gambling account.
  4. Decline any bonus above 25x. The arithmetic is on my casino bonuses page. Above 25x, a bonus costs you money on average and locks your balance while it does so.
  5. Turn off turbo spin and autoplay yourself. The UKGC banned both for good evidence-based reasons. Nothing stops you applying that rule to your own account.
  6. Keep a written record of every deposit across every account, in one place. The specific risk of holding several offshore accounts is losing sight of the total, and a note on your phone solves it.

Questions about non-GamStop casinos

What is a non-GamStop casino?

An online casino without a UK Gambling Commission licence. Participation in GamStop is a condition of that licence, so an operator licensed elsewhere — usually Curaçao or Anjouan — is not connected to the scheme and cannot see a UK self-exclusion. The same absence also removes the £5 slot stake cap, the 2.5-second spin limit, the autoplay ban, affordability checks, segregated customer funds and free binding dispute resolution through IBAS.

Is it illegal to play at a casino not on GamStop?

Not for you. Section 33 of the Gambling Act 2005 makes it an offence to provide unlicensed gambling facilities to consumers in Great Britain, and that offence sits with the operator. There is no offence of using an unlicensed operator and no player has ever been prosecuted. Your winnings also remain free of tax. What you lose is protection rather than legality: no GamStop, no IBAS, no funds segregation requirement and no UKGC stake or speed limits.

Can GamStop see that I played at an offshore casino?

No. GamStop is a database that UK-licensed operators query before opening an account; it does not monitor your activity and has no visibility of sites outside its scheme. That also means it cannot protect you there. If your self-exclusion matters to you — and it did when you set it up — BetBlocker and Gamban both block offshore sites, and your bank's gambling block stops most card deposits at source.

Will my GamStop exclusion end early if I play elsewhere?

No. A GamStop registration runs for its full chosen term of six months, one year or five years and cannot be cancelled early, regardless of what you do at operators outside the scheme. After the term expires the exclusion continues until you actively contact GamStop to reinstate your accounts, and there is a cooling-off period after that request.

Why do non-GamStop casinos offer such large bonuses?

Because they are not bound by UK advertising and bonus rules, and because their acquisition costs are higher — they cannot advertise mainstream in Britain, so they buy attention with the headline figure instead. The size of the number is not the value of the offer. What determines value is the wagering multiple: on my model a 600% bonus at 35x is worth less than a 100% bonus at 10x, and break-even sits at about 25x.

Are non-GamStop casinos safe?

Less protected than a UKGC-licensed site, unambiguously. There is no requirement to segregate customer funds, no free binding complaints route, and recourse in a dispute goes through an offshore regulator that is slow and rarely decisive. What you can check for yourself is a licence that appears on the issuing regulator's own register, published withdrawal limits, and a record of paying. All 74 withdrawals I have requested across these operators were paid, and that data is published in full.

Do non-GamStop casinos ask for ID?

Yes. Anti-money-laundering obligations apply under the licensing regimes these operators hold, and every site I have tested ran identity verification at first withdrawal — photo ID, a selfie and a proof of address dated within three months. What they generally do not run is UKGC-style affordability checking, which is the main reason some players leave the UK market. Upload your documents when you open the account and your first payout will be far quicker.

Can I use a UK debit card at a non-GamStop casino?

Sometimes. Some UK issuers decline transactions to unlicensed gambling merchants outright, and any gambling block you have switched on in your banking app will stop them regardless of the casino. Apple Pay and Google Pay often succeed where the raw card fails. Gambiva supports a genuine open banking transfer from a UK bank app. Cryptocurrency works everywhere and is also the fastest route out, with a median under four hours in my testing.

What should I do if a non-GamStop casino refuses to pay me?

Put the complaint in writing to the operator's formal complaints address rather than live chat, quoting the offer terms as they appeared when you claimed — screenshots matter here. Allow the period their terms specify, usually fourteen days. Then escalate to the regulator named in the site footer, checking first that the licence actually appears on that regulator's register. Be realistic: an offshore regulator is far slower and less decisive than IBAS would be for a UKGC-licensed site, which is one of the genuine costs of playing outside the UK system.

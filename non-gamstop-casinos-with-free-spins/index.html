How to price a free spins offer

Four steps. It takes about thirty seconds once you have done it twice.

The free spins model

1. Notional value = number of spins × the value per spin. Spin value is nearly always £0.10 and occasionally £0.20. It is in the terms, never in the advert. 200 spins at £0.10 is £20 of staking, not £20 of money.

2. Expected winnings = notional value × the slot's RTP. At 96%, 200 spins of £0.10 returns about £19.20 on average — but as bonus funds, not cash.

3. Apply the wagering. If winnings carry 40x, your £19.20 must be turned over £768 before it converts. At a 4% house edge that turnover costs £30.72, which is more than the balance. The realistic conversion rate is low, and most attempts reach zero first.

4. Apply the cap. Even in the minority of cases that convert, a £100 maximum withdrawal caps the result.

Simulated across those conditions, a typical 200-spin welcome offer lands around £5 to £8 of expected value. Worth taking, since it costs nothing. Not worth choosing a casino for.

The exception, and it is a large one: spins whose winnings are paid as cash rather than bonus funds. No wagering, no cap, no discount. Those are genuinely worth their face value and they are rare enough that I name them specifically wherever they appear.

Questions about free spins

What are free spins actually worth?

Far less than the headline. Each spin is nearly always valued at £0.10 in the terms, so 200 spins is £20 of staking rather than £20 of money. At 96% RTP that returns about £19 on average, but as bonus funds carrying 35x to 45x wagering plus a £50 to £100 withdrawal cap. Working that through, a typical 200-spin welcome offer is worth roughly £5 to £8 in expectation. Worth claiming since it costs nothing; not worth choosing a casino for.

It gives 150 spins on the welcome package and then a weekly drop of 30 to 75 spins on named Pragmatic Play and Playson titles, which kept arriving for the eleven weeks I held the account. At roughly £3 to £6 of expected value a week, that compounds to far more than Spin Pin's larger one-off 450 spins. The weekly drop requires a £20 qualifying deposit and the spins expire in 48 hours.

Can you withdraw free spins winnings?

Usually only after clearing wagering on those winnings, typically 35x to 45x, and then only up to a maximum cashout of £50 to £100. The exception is offers that pay spin winnings as cash rather than bonus funds — no wagering, no cap — which are genuinely worth face value and rare enough to be worth seeking out. Always check which structure applies before using the spins, because it changes the value by an order of magnitude.

Do free spins expire?

Yes, and faster than most people expect. Welcome spins typically last seven days; recurring weekly drops often expire in 24 to 48 hours, which means unused spins are simply lost. Some offers also release spins in daily tranches — 20 a day for ten days rather than 200 at once — so missing a day forfeits that day's batch. Both conditions are in the terms and neither appears in the advert.

Which slots can I use free spins on?

Usually one named title, chosen by the operator, and rarely a high-RTP one. The commonest are Big Bass Bonanza, Sweet Bonanza, Gates of Olympus, Book of Dead and Wolf Gold. Be aware that several of these ship in multiple RTP builds — Big Bass Bonanza exists at 96.71% and 95.03%, Book of Dead at 96.21% and 94.25% — so open the game information panel before using the spins to see which build you are on.

Are free spins better than a deposit bonus?

Almost never. A low-wagering cash match is worth several times a spins package: Smash's 10x match on deposit plus bonus returns roughly £80 in expectation on a £100 deposit, against £5 to £8 for a typical 200-spin offer. Spins are a supplement, not a reason to choose a casino. The one exception is a recurring weekly drop, which compounds over a year in a way a one-off match does not.

Do I need a bonus code for free spins?

Sometimes, and a missed code is almost never applied retrospectively. Check the offer page for a code before registering and enter it at the point the terms specify — usually at registration or on the deposit screen, not afterwards. If you realise immediately, live chat will occasionally apply it before you play; once you have staked, they will not.

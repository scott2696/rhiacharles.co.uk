Sending a correction

The more you give me, the faster it gets fixed. Ideally:

  • The page — a URL rather than a description.
  • The claim you believe is wrong, quoted.
  • What it should say, and how you know.
  • Evidence — a screenshot with a visible date, a link to the operator's current terms, a regulator's register entry.

I check every correction against my own records before changing anything, so a correction backed by evidence moves much faster than one backed by an assertion. Once verified, the fix goes up with a dated note on the page it affected, and if it changes a score or a ranking, that goes on the withdrawal ledger with the reason. The full policy is on the about page.

If you have a dispute with a casino

I get this email regularly and I want to be straightforward about what I can and cannot do.

What I cannot do: intervene with an operator on your behalf, escalate a complaint for you, or recover money. I am a publisher with an affiliate relationship, not an ombudsman, and an affiliate leaning on an operator about an individual account would be worth precisely nothing to you and would compromise everything else on this site.

What I can do is tell you the route, which is this:

  1. Complain in writing to the operator's formal complaints address — it is in the terms, and it is not live chat. Set out what happened, with dates, and say what you want.
  2. Attach your evidence. Screenshots of the offer terms as they appeared when you claimed, the transaction history, and any chat transcripts. Terms change; your screenshot does not.
  3. Allow the period their terms specify, usually fourteen days or eight weeks.
  4. Escalate to the licensing regulator named in the site's footer — and check first that the licence actually appears on that regulator's own register, because sometimes it does not.
  5. Be realistic. An offshore regulator is slower and far less decisive than IBAS would be for a UKGC-licensed operator. That absence of recourse is one of the real costs of playing outside the UK system, and it is set out in full on non-GamStop casinos.

If the operator is UK Gambling Commission licensed, your position is much stronger: complain to the operator, allow up to eight weeks, then take it free of charge to its alternative dispute resolution provider, usually IBAS or eCOGRA, whose decision binds the operator.
